Your passion isn't hidden. You build it by trying.

There's no single passion to “discover” once, then a perfect job for life. The sources agree: passion is built through trials — you spot 2–3 leads (what you like + what you're a bit good at + what people need / will pay for), you test them cheaply, you keep the one that still gives you energy after the boring part, then you turn it into a path.

01 — Know yourself

Start with self-knowledge, not a job title

Québec's official approach on “Making a career choice” has four steps: know yourself (questionnaire, RIASEC profile), get advice, discover jobs of the future, and see a guidance counsellor. Page last updated Nov 26, 2024 — quebec.ca/choix-carriere.

Do this first • Free

The RIASEC model — 6 profiles

Six profiles, each linked to abilities, personality traits and interests. The free Québec.ca questionnaire surfaces your three dominant traits and points you to matching jobs of the future. The “One passion, many options” campaign for ages 14–24 is built on the same RIASEC-inspired quiz.

Clarify your non-negotiables

From the Cégep Édouard-Montpetit guide: after interests / traits / aptitudes / values, list your essential criteria — they help you eliminate leads, which is as useful as finding them.

  • Desired length of studies & minimum salary
  • Indoors or not • routine vs. variety
  • Sociable or solitary • manual or intellectual
  • Create or help people

Cégep guidance services offer the same synthesis (interests, skills, traits, values) in 2–5 meetings, with psychometric tests if needed.

Ikigai — a synthesis exercise, not a magic test

Ikigai crosses four circles. The exercise sheet recommends 3–5 points per circle, then spotting the overlaps. Use it to formulate leads — which you then test in Section 2.

Worksheet stays in this tab only — copy it into your notes when you're done. Source: Workforce WindsorEssex Ikigai activity sheet.

3 journaling exercises (15–20 min)

  • Energy journal — 7 days. Each evening, note 2–3 moments: what gave energy vs. drained it, and exactly what you were doing. The decisive test is energy after the session.
  • Curiosity / jealousy journal. Note what sparks curiosity and what makes you a bit jealous — jealousy often hides a desire: “I'd like to be in their place.”
  • 2-year skills inventory. List moments you were absorbed, useful or proud; phrase each strength precisely (“make complicated info understandable in one page” beats “I'm creative”). Ask people around you how they'd describe you — without censoring yourself.

Goal of this step: not one answer, but 2–3 written leads, each scored on energy / current skill / real demand (who would pay, for what concrete benefit?). Keep the top 2–3 totals — a low score just shows what you'd need to learn or accept.

02 — Test cheaply

Test the real work, not the idea of it

Every source shares the same principle: confront the lead with real work. A failed test isn't a failure — it saves you months of training and thousands of dollars in the wrong direction.

Method Cost / time What it reveals Where in Québec
Informational interview Free • 20–30 min Typical day, worst part of the job, real path, surprises Academos (free, 14–30), LinkedIn, alumni, teachers
One-day internship / Student for a day Free • 1 day Real setting, vibe, pace JeunesExplo (14–22), cégep “student for a day”, open houses
Targeted volunteering Free • a few hrs/week Real responsibility, meaning, network Community orgs, camps, schools, CJE
Mini-project / side project Almost free Whether you like doing the work with a client, deadline & revisions Game jam, job for a friend / non-profit, stock simulator
Short intro course / workshop Low cost • a few weeks Whether the subject itself hooks you Cégeps, libraries, free online platforms

Talk to 3–4 people per lead

One opinion can be atypical. Ask about the ordinary, not the exceptional: What does a normal Tuesday look like? What's the worst part? What do people in the field complain about? HEC Montréal stresses: don't ask for a job in an informational meeting. Prepare 5–8 questions, thank them the same day or next, follow up weeks later. Talk to someone early-career and someone experienced.

Deliberately test the boring part

Every job is ~20% what attracted you and 80% everything else. Design: revisions & client feedback. Teaching: grading & paperwork. Liking the 20% proves nothing — tolerating the 80% is a real signal. Also test the lifestyle: hours, solo vs. constant public contact, travel, physical work.

Let the novelty wear off

One day isn't enough — novelty carries anything for a day. Aim for several days spread over a few weeks, or one project from start to finish. Keep a mini-journal during each test: tasks liked / disliked, pace, vibe, mental load, satisfaction. Services Québec may also offer short internships or workplace observation — ask a local office.

A sample 30-day test rhythm from the sources: clarify the goal (days 1–3) → reach out (4–7) → propose a micro-internship (8–14) → do the immersion (15–21) → analyse feedback (22–25) → decide: go deeper, pivot, or plan a gradual transition (26–30).

03 — Turn an interest into a path

The general sequence, then your 4 tracks

1) Validate the lead (Section 2) before any long or costly enrolment. 2) Find the shortest training that opens the door — DEP, technical DEC, certificate, bachelor's — and check prerequisites, limited enrolment & deadlines (often March 1 for limited-enrolment college programs) on Pygma & InforouteFPT. 3) Build a portfolio / proof of work from the test onward. 4) Network continuously. 5) Aim for a first real experience — credited internship, work-study, student job, one-off contract.

None of the four interests below is presented as “the” answer. Each is a lead to test.

🎮
Key question: do you like making games — or only playing them?

Video game creation

Cheap test

Join a game jam (build a small game in a weekend, often in a team, with free tools like Godot or Unity); finish a full tutorial and publish a mini-game on itch.io; interview a developer or 3D artist via Academos. The test must check: coding, modelling, iterating, fixing bugs, working within technical constraints.

Training in Québec

  • DEC Techniques in 3D production & image synthesis (574.C0, 3 years) — e.g. Cégep Limoilou: Unreal Engine, Maya, Blender, Houdini, professional portfolio in session 6, credited internship possible. cegeplimoilou.ca
  • Bachelor's in 3D creation for video games — École NAD-UQAC (Montréal): DEC–BAC bridge (30 credits recognized), portfolio required for admission (minimum 60% evaluation); project-based approach builds your portfolio as you go. uqac.ca/programme/6667

Neighbouring jobs to compare

Game programmer, 3D artist, game designer, tester, animator — compare pay & outlook in the profiles on Québec.ca / Repères.

✏️
Key test: can you produce on command, with deadlines & revisions?

Digital illustration / visual art

Cheap test

Daily drawing challenge for 2–3 weeks (30 min/day, assigned subjects — not only what you like to draw); one real commission for a friend, club or non-profit (poster, logo, illustration) with a deadline and revisions; publish regularly and see if you keep the pace when it's on command.

Training in Québec

  • DEC in Illustration (574.A0, 3 years) — Cégep de Lévis: the only program of its kind in French in Québec; traditional & digital drawing, concept art, characters & environments, 2D animation, running a creative business; exhibition in front of employers + professional portfolio. Admission: portfolio of 20 works + letter of intent (submission March 1 per published criteria). cegeplevis.ca
  • Neighbour: DEC in Graphic design (570.G0), e.g. Dawson — Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign, portfolio.
Heads-up: the admission portfolio requires observational drawing (perspective, portrait, still life). If those exercises completely put you off, that's useful information before you enrol.
🧑‍🏫
Key test: do you like group management & planning — or only the idea of passing things on?

Teaching / recreation work

Cheap test

Lead a group for a few weeks (day camp, homework help, tutoring, student club, youth-centre volunteering) — the most faithful, cheapest test here; observe a day in a classroom or a municipal recreation service; interview a teacher and a recreation technician to compare realities (hours, grading/planning, group management, summers).

Training in Québec

  • Recreation — DEC Techniques in recreation management & intervention (391.A0, 36 months): plan, organize, coordinate, lead & evaluate socio-recreational activities. inforoutefpt.org Work in schools, municipalities, community & recreational tourism; possible continuation in recreology or leisure/culture/tourism at university.
  • Teaching — Bachelor's in preschool & primary education or secondary education (120 credits) leading to a teaching licence. Official list of programs leading to a teaching authorization: Québec.ca
📈
Test this curiosity with no real money first

Finance / trading

Cheap, no-risk test

Open a paper-trading account (virtual money, real prices) and keep a decision journal for 4 weeks; take an intro course in finance / economics; interview a financial advisor, analyst or accountant to distinguish active trading, long-term investing and finance jobs — three different realities.

Québec/Canadian tool: the Montréal Exchange (TMX) trading simulator — free virtual platform, real market data (15-minute delay), without risking a single real dollar; also used in a simulation program for Québec university teams. m-x.ca

Important warnings: Simulators teach mechanics (placing an order, checking quantities) but can encourage risky behaviour — frequent trading, daily checking, trying to “beat the market” — which is closer to gambling than investing; the research cited favours the long term (“buy and hold”) over short-term speculation. Before opening a real account in Canada you need a SIN, a Canadian bank account, and defined goals & risk tolerance. Never borrow, and never use tuition or rent money to “test” trading. Don't confuse one good month in simulation with a stable income. If your interest is really analysis, numbers and advising, the stable jobs in the field (accounting, corporate finance, financial planning, analysis) deserve the same exploration via Repères / Québec.ca. This page gives no financial advice and does not present trading as income.
04 — Common pitfalls

Six traps that waste time & money

1. Confusing a hobby with a job

Loving to cook ≠ running a restaurant with split shifts. Loving animals ≠ physical care, emergencies & clients. Question the real work, not just the subject. If an activity is only enjoyable in its ideal version — no clients, deadlines, repetition or constraints — keep it as a hobby rather than forcing it to pay.

2. Waiting for the perfect passion

“Find your passion” is one of the most time-wasting pieces of advice: it implies a treasure already inside you, so you wait, search, switch programs, and feel behind. Reality is often reversed: passion comes after skill — we love what we do well and are recognized for. Psychology research (O'Keefe, Dweck & Walton, Psychological Science) shows a fixed view of interests makes people less likely to explore and quicker to give up when it gets hard.

3. Believing there's only one right path / it's too late

Four myths slow people down: it's too late to change, you must find your passion first, a perfect path exists, success is measured the same for everyone. Thinking in Plan A (current path) / B (nearby pivot) / Z (fallback, e.g. a food-service job) — from Hoffman & Casnocha, The Start-up of You — releases the pressure of a definitive choice. A vocation is a lifelong process, not a destination.

4. Choosing on a single source

One online test, one video or one relative's opinion isn't enough. The college guide warns: there's “nothing magic” in the tools. Cross-check reliable sources (Repères, Emploi-Québec/IMT, school sites) and validate in the field. Beware prejudices and rumours about jobs, too.

5. Testing everything at once & burning out

Test at most 2 leads in parallel, a few hours a week, while keeping your studies and sleep. Note: this is general prudence drawn from the sources' logic (Plan Z, testing in parallel) — this research verified no burnout figures.

6. Financial & family pressure

Choosing only for salary, or only to please family, without checking whether the daily tasks and lifestyle fit, often ends in dropping out mid-training. That's why personal criteria (§1) and testing the boring part (§2) matter. If money pressure is high, prioritize free tests (RIASEC, Academos, CJE, JeunesExplo, volunteering, simulation) and Québec public training — and talk it through with a guidance counsellor or CJE. That's their job.

06 — Your next 4 weeks

A 4-week exploration plan — 3–5 h/week

Alongside your studies. After the 4 weeks: if one lead stands out, go deeper for 1–2 months (longer project, regular volunteering, intro course) before enrolling in long training. If none stands out, that's not failure — restart the cycle with 2 new leads and talk to a counsellor. The method itself (test, note, adjust) is the skill that eventually finds your path.

Week 1 — Know yourself

Goal: leave with 2–3 written leads

  • Do the Québec.ca RIASEC quiz (jobs of the future); note your 3 dominant traits
  • Fill in your Ikigai (3–5 points per circle)
  • Start the energy journal (5 min each evening)
  • List your non-negotiables (min. salary, study length, hours, solo/group)
Deliverable: One page — RIASEC traits + Ikigai + criteria + 2–3 ranked leads

Week 2 — Explore on paper

Goal: check each lead is a real job

  • Per lead: read 2–3 job profiles on Québec.ca / Repères / MonEmploi (daily tasks, training, salary, outlook)
  • Check training on Pygma / InforouteFPT (length, prerequisites, deadline)
  • Watch 2–3 “Ma formation en vidéo” videos
  • Note per lead: what attracts, what worries, the likely boring part
Deliverable: Comparison table of your 2–3 leads (energy / skill / demand, /5)

Week 3 — Talk & observe

Goal: confront leads with real humans

  • Join Academos; contact 2 mentors (one per top lead)
  • Ask for two 20-min informational interviews (prepared questions: typical day, worst part, path, advice)
  • Book a guidance counsellor or CJE, bringing your Week 2 table
  • Sign up for a JeunesExplo one-day internship or a “student for a day” if dates allow
Deliverable: Notes from 2 interviews + summary of the guidance meeting

Week 4 — Test for real & decide

Goal: do it, don't just read about it

  • Launch one mini-test per top lead — e.g. game jam / finished game tutorial; 7-day drawing challenge + 1 real commission; 2 volunteer leading/tutoring sessions; 1 week of TMX simulation with a decision journal
  • Deliberately do the boring part (revisions, grading/planning, keeping the trading journal)
  • Each evening, note: energy after the session — want to do it again tomorrow? Yes / no / why
Deliverable: Written decision — lead to deepen / keep as a hobby / drop — and the next step (training to verify, portfolio to start, person to recontact)

⚠ Limits & things to recheck

  • JeunesExplo dates: the page consulted listed the day as April 15, 2027, with registration until Dec 6, 2026 (secondary) / Jan 31, 2027 (college). Recheck on jeunes-explorateurs.org before acting.
  • Repères / MonEmploi access: Repères requires access via your school (file number); the exact MonEmploi.com and OCCOQ directory addresses were not live-verified — go via cégep or Québec.ca links.
  • Salaries & outlook vary by region and year — always check the current profile on Québec.ca / Repères / online IMT before deciding.
  • Trading sources are partly commercial; this page gives no financial advice. Simulation with no real money is the only recommended way for a student to test this curiosity.
